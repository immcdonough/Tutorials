5.1 What Is a Bridge Variable?

Bridge variables are computational or behavioral summary statistics that bridge the state-space model to:

  1. Trait-level predictors (Age, depression, memory self-report) — behavioral Stage 2
  2. Neural activity — second-level fMRI regression (who has larger PE beta?)
  3. Connectivity patterns — gPPI and DCM PEB covariates

The design principle: extract model-based quantities from behavior first, test their neural correlates independently. This prevents double dipping.

The pipeline’s power comes from computing bridge variables from behavior before looking at the brain. This prevents circularity and maintains interpretive independence between computation and neural data.








Exercises


1. All second-level models control for Age. When would not controlling for Age be appropriate? Under what circumstances does age-control introduce collider bias?


2. Compute inter-correlations among alpha, transition_rate, and meta_d. Which pairs are collinear enough to cause problems if entered together?


3. In DCM PEB, covariates are centered. Why is centering important? What does the intercept represent in a centered PEB model?
